# Press Release Quote Standard

**Model release:** Molina Healthcare of Arizona + Mountain Park Health Center, Summer Snack Program (June 2026)

## The principle

A quote earns its place only if it says something the body copy cannot.

Neither quote in the AZ release restates the announcement. One explains *why the program is built the way it is*. The other explains *why the plan invested in it*. Strip both quotes out and the release loses real information — that is the test.

The failure mode we're moving away from is the interchangeable quote: a sentence that could appear in any release, from any plan, in any state, with a name swapped. If it survives that swap, it isn't a quote. It's filler.

## The two-quote structure

| | Partner / practitioner quote | Plan executive quote |
|---|---|---|
| **Speaker** | Clinic leader, community org, provider partner | Plan president or line-of-business lead |
| **Job** | How it works on the ground and why it's designed that way | Why we invested and what it's part of |
| **Vantage point** | Inside the exam room, the pantry, the visit | Strategy, portfolio, mission |
| **Ends on** | What changes for the family | What this proves about how we operate |
| **AZ example** | Janey Pearl Starks | Minnie Andrade |

The two quotes must make **different** points. If both say "we're committed to healthy communities," one of them is wasted.

## Deconstructing the AZ quotes

### Partner quote — Janey Pearl Starks, Mountain Park Health Center

> "When children visit us for their wellness checkups, we have a real opportunity to support their health from every angle, including what they eat at home. Our partnership with Molina allows us to put nutritious food directly into the hands of the families who need it most and build healthy habits that will benefit these children long after the summer ends."

- **Sentence 1 justifies the program's central design choice.** The body copy states that participants must complete a wellness visit or weight check. The quote supplies the *reasoning* for that requirement — the visit is the touchpoint that makes the food intervention possible. That rationale appears nowhere else in the release.
- **"When children visit *us*"** places the speaker inside her own operation. She is describing something she does, not endorsing something that happened to her.
- **Sentence 2 names the concrete deliverable** — nutritious food, directly into the hands of families — then extends the timeline past the announcement: *"long after the summer ends."* The quote closes on consequence, not on the program.

### Plan executive quote — Minnie Andrade, Molina Healthcare of Arizona

> "Healthy eating is not just about nutrition. It is the foundation every child needs to grow, thrive, and stay well. This program reflects our whole-person health approach and our belief that every person, family, and community deserves access to the support they need, regardless of their circumstances."

- **Sentence 1 reframes the subject.** It argues that a snack program is a clinical intervention, not a charitable gesture. That reframe is the executive's actual contribution — it tells the reader how to categorize the news.
- **"This program reflects…" is the hinge.** Values language is permitted, but only after it is tethered to the specific investment. The mission statement doesn't float free; it's anchored to a named strategy (whole-person health) and to *this* program.
- **It ends on the eligibility principle** — *regardless of their circumstances* — which is the whole point of a program built for kids who don't qualify for WIC.

## The rules

1. **Lead with the program, not the company.** The first clause should be about the work, the patient, or the problem.
2. **One job per quote.** Assign each speaker a distinct point before drafting. Never let both quotes cover the same ground.
3. **Add information.** Every quote must carry at least one rationale, commitment, or forward-looking claim not present in the surrounding paragraphs.
4. **Name the mechanic.** Wellness checkups. Ages 6 through 17. A $100 monthly Instacart credit. Food lockers. Specifics are what make a quote unfakeable.
5. **Write from the speaker's vantage point.** Clinic leaders speak from exam rooms. Plan presidents speak from strategy. A quote that could belong to either speaker belongs to neither.
6. **End on consequence.** "Long after the summer ends." "Regardless of their circumstances." Not "we look forward to continuing this partnership."
7. **Earn the values line.** Mission language is fine as the *second* half of a quote, attached to a specific with a hinge like "This program reflects…" Never as the opener.
8. **Two sentences. 40–60 words.** Both AZ quotes sit in that range. Anything longer stops being quotable.
9. **Banned openers:** proud, thrilled, excited, honored, pleased, delighted. **Banned self-descriptors:** leading, premier, innovative, best-in-class, cutting-edge, world-class, trusted.
10. **Write for the pull quote.** Assume a reporter takes one sentence. Make sure the sentence they take carries the program, not the adjectives.

## Before and after

**Program launch — before**

> "We are proud to partner with Mountain Park Health Center to support the health and well-being of our members," said [Name]. "At Molina, we are committed to improving the health of the communities we serve."

*Fails on:* no program, no mechanic, no consequence, banned opener, and it works verbatim for any partner in any state.

**Program launch — after:** the Starks quote above.

---

**Benefit expansion — before**

> "This new benefit demonstrates our ongoing commitment to our members and their families."

**Benefit expansion — after**

> "Formula and groceries are the first thing a family cuts when money is tight, and it happens exactly when a pregnancy needs the most nutrition. A $100 monthly credit removes that tradeoff for the months it matters most."

*Works because:* it names the behavior it's correcting, names the mechanic and the amount, and ends on the member's decision rather than the company's intent.

---

**Community investment — before**

> "We are excited to announce this investment, which underscores our leadership in addressing social determinants of health."

**Community investment — after**

> "Our providers kept telling us the same thing: patients were leaving appointments with a plan they couldn't follow because there was no fresh food within two miles of home. Putting a food locker at the clinic closes the distance between the advice and the groceries."

*Works because:* it sources the problem to a real signal, quantifies it, and explains what the investment physically changes.

## Intake questions before you draft

Substantive quotes require substance. Ask these before writing a word — the answers *are* the quote.

**For the partner**
- What happens in your clinic or office that made this program necessary?
- Why is the program designed this way? (Ask about every requirement, eligibility rule, and date.)
- What does a family actually receive, and when?
- What do you expect to be different for these families a year from now?

**For the plan executive**
- What did we see in the data — or hear from providers — that made this a priority?
- What larger strategy is this one piece of? What came before it and what comes next?
- What did we commit: dollars, duration, reach?
- Who does this reach that nothing else reaches?

## Pre-send checklist

- [ ] Swap in another state and another partner name. Does the quote still work? If yes, rewrite it.
- [ ] Does the quote name at least one concrete mechanic of the program?
- [ ] Do the two quotes make genuinely different points?
- [ ] Does either quote open with proud / excited / thrilled / honored?
- [ ] Does each quote carry something not already in the body copy?
- [ ] Does each quote end on an outcome for members, not on the announcement?
- [ ] Read it aloud. Does it sound like this person, in this role?

## What the release did *around* the quotes

Substantive quotes need a release that gives them something to be substantive about. The AZ release supplied:

- **Hard numbers.** 240 members applied in Summer 2025; approximately 95 completed.
- **Precise eligibility.** Ages 6 through 17, targeted at children who don't qualify for WIC.
- **An origin story.** "Rooted in conversations around healthy food choices and diabetes prevention."
- **A portfolio paragraph.** The NOAH Fresh for You food lockers and the Healthy Moms Healthy Babies $100 Instacart credit, establishing this as one move in a sustained pattern rather than a one-off.
- **A real call to action.** Contact a Mountain Park Health Center clinic directly.

Hold future releases to the same standard. A quote can only be as specific as the release around it.
